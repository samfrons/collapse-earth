Collapse Earth

Sixteen Earth systems have thresholds. Not one of them is a date, and not one is a single number — each is a range with a confidence rating attached. This atlas holds those ranges beside the money now moving in climate technology, and asks why the two do not line up.

    The thresholds

    Every threshold here is a range, not a line. Move the dial and each element lights in proportion to how far into its own uncertainty range the world has travelled. Nothing on this globe flips at a single number, because the science does not.

    Threshold values, central estimates and confidence ratings are all from one table — Armstrong McKay et al., Science (2022), Table 1. Crossing a range is not tipping: it starts a process whose expression takes the timescale named in each dossier, from a decade to ten millennia.

      Act II

      The misallocation

      Capital can be counted. Leverage cannot. This act keeps them in separate columns: every dollar figure carries the badge its source used and the date it was current, and no two kinds of money are ever added together.

      Where capital is most missing

      How the capital column is built
      A funding list like this one is not summable as it stands. It holds six kinds of money — capital raised, capital being sought, offtake contracts, prizes, grants, and sector-level roll-ups that already contain the company rows beneath them. The capital figure on each row adds only entries marked as capital, and drops any entry the data flags as sitting inside a cumulative total, so nothing is counted twice. Contracts, prizes, grants and targets appear as themselves, never folded into the total. Where a source gave no figure, the row says so rather than showing zero.
