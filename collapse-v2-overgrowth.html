The Misallocation

Leverage and capital are not the same measure, so they are not drawn on one axis. Capital here means equity and debt actually raised. Offtake contracts, prizes, grants, capital being sought, and sector roll-ups are held separately and labelled as what they are, and any row that sits inside a company's own cumulative total is excluded from the sum. The right-hand column is an editorial assessment, not a measurement.

Methods & sources

How to read this, and where it comes from

This is a research position, not investment advice.

Sources

Every figure on this page carries a source and a date. Citations marked verify are real and dated but not yet re-confirmed; where no stable link exists, none is invented.

    Rules the figures follow

    Funding is never summed across kinds. A capital total adds equity and debt only, and excludes any row that already sits inside a company's cumulative figure. Offtake contracts are customer commitments, prizes are prizes, grants are grants, capital being sought is a target, and sector roll-ups already contain the company rows beneath them.

    Announced is never rendered as delivered. Every money figure shows its badge and its as of date exactly as recorded — including mixed granularity such as a quarter or a half-year, which is left as written rather than rounded into a false precision.

    The gap score is an assessment. It is an editorial 1–5 read of how far potential impact runs ahead of capital. It is labelled as an assessment everywhere it appears and is not a measured quantity.

    An absence of funding is only claimed where a source claims it. Where a company is reported to have no venture funding, the source's own words are quoted. A source being silent is not the same as a source reporting none.

    Thresholds are ranges. Minimum, central and maximum with a confidence rating, from Armstrong McKay et al. (2022), Table 1. The globe and the band chart both render partial crossing across the range; neither flips at the central value.
