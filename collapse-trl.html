The Bench

Every intervention category on the lead page prints one number for readiness: a TRL band. This page is the instrument that number came out of — where the scale comes from, what evidence each rung actually demands, what the number silently drops, and then, category by category, the subsystem readiness, engineering constraint and supply chain underneath each band.

Bands and maturity statements are read live from the shared data layer (collapse-data.v2.js) — the same file the lead page renders from. Nothing on this page restates a band by hand: every dossier is checked against the data layer at load, and a mismatch renders a visible failure stamp rather than a silently wrong number.

§B1 · the scale

Nine rungs, one question each

Technology Readiness Levels began as an internal NASA discipline in the 1970s, were formalised as a seven-level scale in 1989, and reached their canonical nine-level form in John Mankins' 1995 white paper. The US Department of Defense adopted the scale for acquisition in the early 2000s, ISO 16290 fixed the space definitions in 2013, and the European Commission generalised the nine levels to all technologies in Horizon 2020 (2014). The IEA later stretched the ladder to eleven levels for energy technology, because in energy a working product is not the end of the story — integration and proof of stability at scale are rungs of their own.

Each rung is best read as a question the evidence has to answer, not a grade. The examples in the right column are drawn from this atlas's own dataset.

rungwhat it assertsevidence standardfrom this dataset
TRL 1Basic principles observed Peer-reviewed science says the mechanism exists. Tropospheric chlorine chemistry oxidises methane — the premise behind iron-salt-aerosol proposals.
TRL 2Concept formulated A paper design applies the mechanism to a use; no experimental proof of that application. Open-atmosphere methane removal concepts — modelled, never isolated in the open atmosphere (dossier §1).
TRL 3Proof of concept Critical function demonstrated analytically or experimentally at lab bench. Microbial electrosynthesis to carboxylic acids — assessed at TRL 3/4, lab scale (Trends in Biotechnology, 2024).
TRL 4Validated in the lab Components work together in a low-fidelity breadboard. The MES frontier's current ceiling: "requiring process optimisation before an industrial prototype" (dossier §7).
TRL 5Validated in relevant environment The breadboard survives realistic conditions — real seawater, real barn air, real soil. Ambient Carbon's MEPS container on a 250-cow dairy barn, removing up to 90% of methane (2025).
TRL 6Prototype demonstrated in relevant environment A system-level prototype, not a component rig, performs in the field. Boston Metal's 1+ ton of steel/iron at pilot scale via molten oxide electrolysis (2025).
TRL 7Prototype demonstrated in operational environment Near-final scale and form, operating where it will actually be used. DAC's commercially operating plants — at roughly ten times the target cost (dossier §5).
TRL 8System complete and qualified The actual system, proven through test and demonstration; first-of-kind commercial units. Biochar: commercially operating at scale, permanence assurance still the maturing edge (dossier §6).
TRL 9Proven in successful operations Repeated, routine commercial operation — and in energy, bankability: lenders price it. Enhanced geothermal: IPO plus the first non-recourse project debt in the field's history (dossier §9a).

Wording above is condensed from the EC Horizon 2020 General Annex G definitions, which generalise NASA's; the space-heritage originals are in ISO 16290:2013 and Mankins (1995). The IEA's extension adds TRL 10 (commercial and competitive, still needing integration effort) and TRL 11 (proof of stability reached) — a reminder that for climate technology, rung 9 is a beginning.

§B2 · the silences

Four things a TRL will not tell you

TRL measures demonstration maturity — how far the evidence chain runs from principle to routine operation. It is silent about almost everything an investor, an engineer or a policymaker also needs. Four companion axes exist precisely because of those silences, and this page's dossiers report against all four.

cost — no axis at all

A technology can hold TRL 7 while costing ten times its viability target — that is DAC's exact position in this dataset: commercially operating plants, ≈$600–1,000/t against a ≈$100/t target. TRL counts working plants, not working economics.

manufacturability — MRL

The US DoD keeps a separate ten-level Manufacturing Readiness Level scale because a one-off prototype proves nothing about producing at rate. In this dataset the gap is visible as contracted-versus-delivered tonnage: DAC has delivered ≈0.05% of what it has contracted.

adoption — ARL / CRI

The US DOE's Adoption Readiness Levels (2022) and ARENA's Commercial Readiness Index (2014) score demand, value proposition and market openness. Blue carbon shows why: the ecology is mature, yet only ≈10 of ≈81 projects were actively issuing credits as of Oct 2025.

the valley — TRL 4–7

The well-documented "valley of death": too applied for research grants, too unproven for project finance. §B6 below plots this dataset's own capital against the rungs — the trough in the middle of the ladder is not a metaphor, it is the shape of the data.

two structural critiques worth carryingclose

System versus component. A TRL attaches to a specific system in a specific environment. Quoting one number for a category containing many subsystems silently takes either the best or the worst of them. This is the failure the data layer calls out by name in the microbial-electro entry — "conflating them is the field's main honesty failure" — and it is why every dossier below decomposes its category into subsystem rows rather than defending a single rung.

No downgrade convention. TRL practice has no standard way to move a technology down the ladder when evidence turns against it. This dataset handles that editorially: ice intervention is banded at its evidence (TRL 1), not its hardware trials, because the two most recent independent assessments are negative. Where the prose hedges, the band goes down — never up.

§B3 · house rules

How this atlas assigns a band

The lead page's assay field sizes its markers by trlBand, an integer 1–9 stored in the shared data layer. The rules it is assigned under are written into that file as load-bearing comments; the operative ones are quoted here verbatim, because they are the method:

"Both are editorial ordinals. They are bands, not measurements, and they must never be presented on a continuous numeric axis, averaged, summed, differenced, or quoted as a quantity."

"trlBand — integer 1–9, the approximate TRL midpoint of the maturity string. Where maturity gives a range, the midpoint is used and a midpoint falling on a half rounds down: this page never rounds readiness upward."

"Two categories state a genuinely wide internal spread … collapsing those to one number loses real information, so the per-entry comment records what was collapsed and the full maturity string stays the authority."

collapse-data.v2.js — interventions preamble

Three consequences follow, and this page inherits all of them. First, the charts here place bands on discrete rungs only — no continuous TRL axis exists anywhere on this site. Second, rounding is asymmetric by design: a category stating "TRL 6–7" is banded 6, not 7. Third, for the two categories with a wide internal spread, the single band is the least informative number on the page — which is exactly why the dossiers below exist.

§B4 · the ladder

Every stated maturity span, on nine rungs

Each row is one intervention category. The bars are the TRL spans the data layer's maturity strings actually state — including the internal route spreads the single band collapses. The diamond is the stored band (midpoint, rounded down). Categories are ordered by band, least ready first.

Rungs are ordinal — the horizontal spacing carries no interval meaning, and no value between rungs exists. Where a category shows more than one bar, its maturity string names distinct routes at genuinely different stages; the thin connector marks the span the stored band is the midpoint of. Open the category's dossier for the decomposition. One honest limit of the consistency gate: it verifies this chart transcribes the data layer's recorded readings faithfully — it cannot arbitrate which rung a hedged word like "commercial" deserves, and that call differs by entry (the data layer's own comments fix each one; they are the authority followed here).

§B5 · the dossiers

Ten categories, taken apart

Each dossier decomposes its category into subsystem readiness rows, then reports the engineering constraint that gates scale-up, the supply chain with its chokepoints flagged, the binding constraint holding the band where it is, and the one measurement that would move it a rung. Register, stated once for all ten: subsystem rows and supply-chain reads are editorial engineering assessments in the same sense as the data layer's bands — the argument is in the prose, the row is a handle on it. Quantities appear only where the data layer or its named sources state them; everything else is deliberately qualitative.

§B6 · capital against the rungs

Where the money sits on the ladder

Disclosed capital actually raised per category — summed under the data layer's own rules (equity or debt with stream: "capital", excluding every row inside another row's cumulative total; offtakes, prizes, grants, targets and sector roll-ups never counted) — placed at each category's band. Hedges survive the sum: a ≥ means at least one entry is a floor or an amount is undisclosed, and a blank is not a zero.

bedrock · methods & sources

Methods & sources

Bands, maturity strings, funding entries, delivered tonnages and caveats are read at load from collapse-data.v2.js, the shared data layer of this site, whose source of record for funding and company claims is the internal research synthesis (collapse-tech-research.md, July 2026). The consistency gate on this page re-derives every dossier's band from its stated route spans under the house rules (§B3) and compares the result against the data layer; a disagreement renders a visible failure stamp on the dossier.
