§P1 · the mandate

The growth window, drawn on the ladder

The role's posting defines its own scope, and the definition is a readiness claim — so it can be drawn on the same nine rungs as everything else on this site:

"The position focuses on evaluating growth-stage climate companies (Technology Readiness Level 6–8) through technical diligence … define critical technical questions for investments, prioritize risks, and identify information gaps that would affect investment decisions … assessments translating technical uncertainty into capital decision implications … failure modes and cost-reliability tradeoffs."

Lowercarbon Capital, Technical Lead – Growth posting (lowercarbon-capital.breezy.hr/p/49a05e59169a, read 2026-08-10)

TRL 6–8 is a precise place to sit. Below it, diligence is science review; above it, it is project finance. Inside it live the questions this atlas's Bench calls the mid-ladder's own: does the prototype's performance survive the move to the operational environment, does the cost curve bend before the capital runs out, and does the verification subsystem mature as fast as the deployment subsystem. §P5 · the portfolio-level reads

What the shape of the book says

a barbell, with the window in the middle

The book is heavy at the frontier — eight distinct fusion bets (magnets, stellarators, pulsed, laser, Z-pinch, compact) that are TRL 2–4 science positions — and heavy in software and finance rails (Watershed, Crux, Eli, Enode) where TRL barely applies. The physical middle — Antora, Twelve, Electra, Sublime, Heirloom, Charm, Zanskar — is exactly the growth window this role underwrites. The barbell is a choice: cheap options on physics, cash-flowing rails, and concentrated technical risk in between.

the fund builds its own verification layer

Isometric (registry), Yard Stick (soil carbon measurement), Loam (farm-to-registry), CarbonPlan and Carbon180 (nonprofit science and policy): the book funds the measurement infrastructure its own removal companies will be priced by. This atlas's Bench argues MRV is the binding constraint in four of ten categories; the book reads as if the fund agrees, and is long the constraint itself.

everything in the window buys electrons

Antora arbitrages them, Twelve electrolyses them, Electra plates iron with them, Heirloom and Sublime consume them, Zanskar produces them. The dominant shared exposure across the profiled eight is delivered clean electricity price — which makes the cost-reliability tradeoff the posting names the portfolio's single most repeated diligence question, and interconnection queues its quietest schedule risk.

the window's recurring failure mode is FOAK finance

Sublime's paused Holyoke plant (rescinded $87M DOE grant) is the live case; Electra's first commercial facility and Twelve's next AirPlants are the next tests. In this window, technology risk and financing risk are the same risk wearing different badges — the diligence question is rarely "does it work" and usually "what evidence unlocks the next tranche of someone else's capital".
