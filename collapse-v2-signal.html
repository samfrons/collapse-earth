How to read
this instrument

Four rules govern every mark on the page. They exist so that a measurement, a claim and an opinion can never be mistaken for one another — which, in a dataset that mixes a peer-reviewed threshold table with press releases, is the whole job.

01 — One colour axis

Confirmed · measured · below range
Claimed · announced · inside range
Exceeded · falsified · above range

Hue is used for exactly one thing on this page: how settled a number is. Category is drawn as a shape, never a colour. Every coloured mark is also labelled in words, so nothing depends on seeing the difference.

02 — Crossing fraction

ƒ = (T − min) ÷ (max − min), clamped 0…1

A threshold is a published range, not a date. Rather than flip a system from “safe” to “tipped” at its central estimate, the instrument reports how far into its uncertainty range the dial has travelled. A bar half-lit means the dial sits halfway between the low and high estimates.

ƒ is not a probability. It is a position inside an interval. Nothing here estimates the odds of a tipping point.

03 — Confidence is texture

High confidence — solid
Medium — broken
Low — hatched, noisy

The confidence rating is the source table's own, not ours. Noisier bars are less certain bars; the loudest, cleanest bars are the ones the literature agrees on. Most of this table is low confidence, and it looks like it.

04 — Opinion never glows

Editorial — dotted, unlit

Measured quantities are drawn as solid, luminous marks. Editorial judgements — chiefly the 1–5 funding-gap assessment in Act II — are drawn dotted, hatched and without glow, and are labelled as assessments wherever they appear.

Money is never summed across kinds. Capital raised, purchase contracts, prizes, grants and sector roll-ups are separate streams, shown separately, each with the badge and asOf date it was published with. Dates keep the granularity of their source — a quarter stays a quarter.

Why the bar is only ever capital

The funding array in the data layer deliberately holds money that cannot be added together: equity and debt actually raised, capital being sought, offtake contracts, prize awards, grants deployed, and sector-level roll-ups that already contain the company rows beneath them.

So the bar sums one stream — capital, excluding rows marked as sitting inside a cumulative total. Every other stream appears as a labelled chip with its own badge and date. A category with a short bar and three grant chips is not unfunded; it is funded in a way that does not buy scale.

How thresholds are computed

Threshold minimum, central estimate, maximum and confidence rating are taken unaltered from Armstrong McKay et al. 2022, Table 1, which is the authority for the whole tipping-system table. Nothing is interpolated, averaged across sources, or rounded.

State at a given dial setting T is: below when T < min, in range when min ≤ T ≤ max, and above when T > max. Crossing fraction ƒ = (T − min) / (max − min), clamped to 0…1, is a position inside the published interval — not a likelihood, and not a forecast.

The band chart's axis spans the full extent of the table, so no range is clipped — clipping a published maximum would misstate the uncertainty, which is the one thing this page must not do. The dial, however, stops short of that extent, so part of every affected row lies outside what the control can reach. — That region is hatched on the chart, and each affected row states the highest crossing fraction the dial can produce for it, so an unlit bar there reads as beyond the control's range rather than as not crossed.

How money is counted

Capital raised for a category sums only rows whose stream is capital and which are not marked as sitting inside another row's cumulative total. Targets, offtake contracts, prizes, grants and sector-level aggregates are never added to it and never drawn as part of the same bar.

Each row keeps the badge it was published with — announced, verified, delivered, deployed-grants — and its asOf date at the granularity the source used. An announcement is never rendered as a delivery. Hedges in the source (“more than”, “approximately”) are preserved as qualifiers.

The 1–5 funding-gap figure is an editorial assessment by the authors of the research synthesis, not a measurement. It is drawn dotted and unlit for that reason, and never as a bar.

What the footage is

The moving imagery is instrument texture, not evidence. The hero plate and the auxiliary feed are NASA Scientific Visualization Studio renders, used at the resolution and frame rate NASA publishes them at — in one case six frames per second, because the underlying series is a two-decade satellite record, not video.

They are presented behind scan-line, grain and scrim layers so that the low frame rate and soft resolution read as what they are: a downlinked feed. No clip is stretched and sharpened to pretend otherwise. Credits and licences for every entry in the media manifest are listed below, whether or not this page uses them.

What this page is not

It is not a forecast. No date on which any system tips appears anywhere, because the source table does not provide one. —

It is not a screen, a ranking, or a portfolio. The company atlas reproduces the research synthesis's tiers and one fact each; it does not score them.

It is not advice.
